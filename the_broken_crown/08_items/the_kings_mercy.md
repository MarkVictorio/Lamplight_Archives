# The King’s Mercy

*Shattered sword of the Crown’s “mercy.”*

**Kind:** Artifact (fragmented)  
**Era:** The Crown  
**Condition:** Broken — scattered pieces; full form unknown in the modern age  
**Public footprint:** Rarely named aloud; records **suppressed**, **edited**, or **lost** with Crown fall  

**Sources:** [Campaign Overview](../00_core/Overview.md) (Crown, Thralls, seals) · [Circle of the Spire](../02_factions/Circle%20of%20the%20Spire.md) (post-Crown custody rumors) · Play: [Session 1](../06_sessions/session_01.md), [Session 3](../06_sessions/session_03.md), [Session 6](../06_sessions/session_06.md), [Session 8](../06_sessions/session_08.md)

## What it was

**King’s Mercy** was a **great blade** of the Crown’s wars of **unification and pacification** — a weapon carried in legend by champions of the throne and raised over **subdued cities**, **broken keeps**, and **rebels** who would not kneel.

The name was **propaganda**: “mercy” meant **swift capitulation** or **exemplary violence**. Court poets called it deliverance; border chronicles and confessional letters describe **massacres**, **reprisals against civilians**, and **terror** used to weld the kingdom into one obedience.

It was **not** solely a vampire-slaying sword. The Crown’s enemies included **undead**, **cults**, **foreign hosts**, and **ordinary people** who happened to be in the way. That breadth is what stains the Mercy’s memory: **innocent blood** is not a footnote in the surviving fragments — it is **structural** to how the blade earned its dread.

---

## Tainted history

Modern reactions vary by who learns the truth:

- **Last Ember** clergy and compassionate orders often find the Mercy **obscene** — a relic of “light” used to **burn** as much as to save.  
- **Scholars** argue whether it was **cursed**, **blessed**, or **neither** — only **saturated** with intent and death.  
- **Noble houses** with Crown-era roots may **deny**, **hide**, or **covet** fragments for **legitimacy** or **blackmail**.

Treat the Mercy as **politically radioactive** as often as “powerful”: owning a piece can mean **guilt by association** or **leverage** over those who fear what it remembers.

---

## Why it shattered (competing theories)

No single account dominates. Use whichever fits your table, or let PCs uncover contradictions.

1. **Ritual sundering** — Defeated loyalists or foreign priests **broke** the sword so no heir could **reclaim** the Crown’s full martial symbol; pieces were **scattered** or **sealed** with old rites.  
2. **The victors’ choice** — Early **Circle** founders (or their agents) **destroyed** or **partitioned** it to prevent any one faction from **marching** under the old standard again; some pieces may sit in **vaults** under **Ravencroft** or **Valemyr** rumor.  
3. **Accident or backlash** — The blade **failed** during a massive working (siege, binding, or **Thrall**-adjacent operation), **shattering** under strain or **counter-magic**; fragments retained **charge** without purpose.

## Fragments

A **fragment** is any **physically distinct** piece still carrying **metal, inscription, or binding** from the original: tang shard, **hilt collar**, **chapel** of the crossguard, **runed** splinter, etc. Not every old sword chip qualifies — only pieces **recognized** by resonance, **text**, or **expert** identification.

Demerin researchers confirm the sword remains **incomplete**. A **clear diamond-shaped gemstone** occupied the final slot. Without it, full restoration is impossible. **Record Keeper Joren** offered future assistance at **Achroma** (*"just ask for Joren"*).

## Properties (narrative and optional 5e hooks)

Full mechanics are optional; the item should **feel** wrong before it feels heroic.

**Atmosphere**

- Carriers report **whispers** of **drums**, **fire**, or a voice urging **one more stroke** — “finish it.”  
- **Last Ember** symbols or **Keeper**-blessed ground may **flicker** or **dim** near a fragment (judgment, not automatic smiting).  
- **Sleep**: fragmented **battle** dreams; waking with **tight grip** on the shard or **blood** under nails without a wound.

**Secondary undead angle**

- The Crown fought **many** threats; a fragment might **bite** harder against **Thralls**, **bound dead**, or **seal-touched** creatures — not because the Mercy was “holy,” but because it was **soaked in the same era’s** binding and **unbinding**. Use sparingly so vampires stay **one** horror among many.

**Mechanics:** See [5e magic item statistics](#5e-magic-item-statistics-player-facing-future) below.

### Shard of the King’s Mercy

*Wondrous item, rare (requires attunement)*

This jagged splinter of silvered steel is heavy for its size. Crown-era etchings appear only when the metal is warm or smeared with blood. A creature that succeeds on an Intelligence (History) check (DC 18) or casts *identify* on the shard learns it is **evocation** and **necromancy** magic in uneasy overlap.

**Bonded weapon (optional downtime).** If you spend **25 gp** in materials and **5 days** working with a smith proficient with smith’s tools, you can mount the shard in the hilt or forte of one **nonmagical** melee weapon that lacks the **light** property. That weapon becomes a **magic weapon** with a **+1** bonus to attack and damage rolls while you remain attuned to the shard (the shard is not destroyed; it can be removed only by you over 8 hours of work, or by *dispel magic* cast at 6th level or higher on the weapon). If the bond ends, the weapon loses this property unless it was already magical.

**Mercy’s verdict.** The first time each turn you hit a creature with your bonded weapon (or, if you have not bonded a weapon, with **any** weapon while this shard is **on your person**), you can deal an extra **1d6** damage of the same type as the weapon’s damage. If the target is a **construct** or **undead**, the extra damage is **force** instead.

**Tithe of the innocent.** The first time each **long rest** you use Mercy’s verdict to deal the extra damage to a creature that has **not** attacked you or a friendly creature since your last long rest, you take **1d6 psychic** damage immediately after the attack resolves (the blade remembers).

**Whispers of the line.** While attuned, you have **advantage** on Charisma (Intimidation) checks made to demand **surrender** or **obedience** in a language you understand. Keepers of the Last Ember who recognize the shard’s nature may react with **hostility** or **fear** at the DM’s discretion.

### King’s Mercy (unfinished)

*Weapon (greatsword), very rare (requires attunement)*

A smith — willing, coerced, or devout — has **folded Crown shards** into new steel and ground a **rough** greatsword: the edge **wavers**, **dark seams** show where pieces refused to marry, and the crossguard is **plain** or **mismatched**. It is unmistakably a **weapon**, not a curio, but anyone with **Crown lore** can tell the work is **incomplete**. (*Identify* still shows **evocation** and **necromancy**; History DC 16 recalls “Mercy” propaganda.)

You gain a **+1** bonus to attack and damage rolls with this magic greatsword.

**Mercy’s verdict.** The first time each turn you hit a creature with this sword, you can deal an extra **1d6** slashing damage as the jagged geometry **catches**. If the target is a **construct** or **undead**, that extra damage is **force** instead.

**Tithe of the innocent.** The first time each **long rest** you use Mercy’s verdict against a creature that has **not** attacked you or a friendly creature since your last long rest, you take **1d6 psychic** damage when the attack resolves.

**Unfinished will.** When you roll a **natural 1** on an attack roll with this weapon, you take **1d4 psychic** damage and cannot use Mercy’s verdict again until the **end** of your next turn (the blade **spasms** wrong).

**Whispers of the line.** Same as the **shard**: advantage on Charisma (Intimidation) to demand **surrender** or **obedience** in a language you understand.

**Growing whole (DM).** If the party later obtains the **remaining** fragments and completes the **legendary** reforging ritual, you can **transform** this weapon **in place** into [The King’s Mercy (reforged)](#the-kings-mercy-reforged) without breaking attunement, or retire it for a new blade at your table’s preference.

**Variant.** For a narrower early-game profile, treat this as a **longsword** instead of a greatsword (same rarity and properties, **versatile 1d10**).

### The King’s Mercy (reforged)

*Weapon (greatsword), legendary (requires attunement by a creature the DM approves)*

You gain a **+3** bonus to attack and damage rolls made with this magic greatsword. It sheds **dim light** in a 10-foot radius; the light is warm amber, not holy.

**Crown’s reach.** When you hit a creature with this weapon, you can choose to deal **necrotic** damage instead of slashing for that hit.

**Pacification.** Once per turn, when you hit a creature with an attack using this sword, you can force the target to make a **DC 18 Wisdom** saving throw. On a failed save, it is **frightened** of you until the end of your next turn. Constructs and undead have **disadvantage** on this save.

**Mercy’s price.** Each time you reduce a creature to 0 hit points with this sword, you take **2d6 psychic** damage (irreducible by resistance). If the creature was **not** hostile toward you when combat began, the damage becomes **4d6**.

**Destroying the Mercy.** The reforged blade cannot be destroyed by mundane means. Sundering it again might require a **specific ritual**, **artifact**, or **divine** intervention — left for your endgame.

## Why Eldros cares

In [Session 3](../06_sessions/session_03.md), **Eldros** suggests the party may **already hold fragments** of King’s Mercy.

**Primary read (default):** He treats fragments as **political and ritual leverage** — tools that could **reforge** a symbol of **Crown violence**, **disrupt** a **seal** working, or **bind** something that remembers the old wars. He benefits from knowing **who** holds them and whether they understand what they carry.

**Alternates (DM)**

- **Fear of reforging** — A whole Mercy might **shift** the balance among Thralls, **New Bloods**, and the **Last Vampire’s** seal; he wants pieces **lost** or **controlled**.  
- **Misidentification** — He is **wrong** or **testing** the party; the shard is **not** Mercy (retcon only if you reject Session 1 link).  
- **Bargaining chip** — He offers **truth** or **safety** in exchange for **surrendering** or **swearing** over a fragment.

## At the table

- Let PCs **discover** the name before the **full** atrocity; **incremental** horror fits the campaign tone.  
- Tie fragments to **investigation** (archives, house sigils, **Ashcomb** ritual notes) more than random loot.  
- If the party **reassembles** the Mercy, that should be a **major** arc — not a weekend crafting project.
