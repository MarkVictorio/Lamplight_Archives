# Religions of the Kingdom

Faith in the kingdom is divided among several quiet traditions rather than a single dominant church. These beliefs rarely clash openly, but each carries its own interpretation of the past and the unseen forces that shape the world.

Many scholars believe that modern religions formed **after the fall of the Crown**, each attempting to interpret fragments of older truths.

Some see divine presence in the world.

Others believe these traditions are merely ways to understand forces humanity cannot control.

---

# The Last Ember
*(Light, Forge)*

The Church of the Last Ember is the most visible and organized faith across the kingdom.

Its central belief is that the world survives by **a single remaining spark of sacred fire**. This ember represents endurance, vigilance, and the quiet duty to keep darkness from returning.

Followers believe the ancient world nearly collapsed into shadow. Only a final divine flame remained, and humanity has guarded it ever since.

The Ember is not worshipped as a god in a traditional sense. Instead it is treated as a **sacred principle** that must be preserved.

### Core Beliefs

• Light must always be maintained  
• Civilization survives through vigilance  
• Darkness returns when people grow careless  
• The faithful must carry the ember forward

### Practices

Churches are built around **central braziers or lantern fires** that are never allowed to extinguish.

Common rituals include:

• Lighting communal lanterns at dusk  
• Blessing tools of craft and labor  
• Rekindling dying fires during ceremonies  
• Maintaining watch through the night

### Clergy

Clergy of the Ember are known as **Keepers**.

They often serve as:

• town priests  
• blacksmith-blessers  
• watchkeepers during the night  
• guardians against occult threats

Their temples are modest but always warm with firelight.

# The Weeping Mother
*(Life)*

The Weeping Mother represents grief, birth, and the quiet endurance of suffering.

Her followers believe the world continues not because suffering is absent, but because **someone remembers the pain of others**.

Shrines often contain statues of a veiled woman whose face cannot be seen beneath flowing stone.

Followers commonly serve as:

• healers  
• midwives  
• caretakers of the dying

Her temples are places where grief is allowed to exist openly.

---

# The Thorn Father
*(Nature, Tempest)*

The Thorn Father represents the untamed wilderness and the violent balance of nature.

Followers believe civilization grows only because the wild allows it, and that storms and forests serve as reminders of the world's older order.

His symbols often include:

• thorned branches  
• storm-carved stones  
• antlers or roots

Shrines are rarely built indoors. Most stand within forests, hilltops, or ruined structures reclaimed by nature.

---

# The Black Tithe
*(Trickery, Death — forbidden sects)*

The Black Tithe is not a formal religion but a **secretive doctrine** whispered about among occult circles.

Its followers believe that **every life must eventually be paid back to the world**, and that death is a debt rather than an end.

Rumors claim its practitioners collect spiritual "tithes" through deception, sacrifice, or manipulation.

Most kingdoms outlaw any open practice of the Black Tithe.

Scholars debate whether the tradition is truly ancient or merely a corruption of older beliefs.

---

# The Crowned Witness
*(Knowledge, Order)*

The Crowned Witness represents truth, memory, and the act of observation.

Followers believe that **nothing in history truly disappears so long as someone remembers it**.

They often serve as:

• historians  
• judges  
• archivists  
• record keepers

Their temples resemble libraries more than churches.

Some claim the Witness sees through **every written word and sworn oath**.

---

# The Silent Choir
*(Knowledge, Twilight)*

The Silent Choir believes truth reveals itself through **quiet reflection rather than spoken doctrine**.

Followers rarely preach. Instead they meditate, observe, and record what they see.

Members often wear simple grey robes and vow long periods of silence.

Their shrines are quiet halls filled with candlelight and written observations.

The Choir teaches that **truth is easiest to hear when the world stops speaking**.

---

# The Pale Shepherd
*(Death, Grave)*

The Pale Shepherd guides the dead toward whatever lies beyond the veil.

Followers believe death is neither punishment nor tragedy. It is simply the moment when every soul is gathered and led onward.

Clergy often serve as:

• grave wardens  
• funeral priests  
• guardians of burial grounds

Their symbols include shepherd crooks, lanterns, and pale stone markers.

Many villages trust them to ensure **the dead remain properly at rest**.

---

# The Gilded Throne
*(War, Order)*

The Gilded Throne represents authority, conquest, and the belief that power must be held by those strong enough to maintain it.

Historically, many noble houses supported this faith.

Its followers believe civilization exists because **someone has the will to rule it**.

Temples often resemble fortified halls or military chapels.

While less common among common folk, the Gilded Throne still holds influence among soldiers, nobles, and rulers.

---

# Religious Landscape

Most citizens follow **multiple traditions simultaneously**.

A person might:

• pray to the Last Ember for protection  
• seek the Weeping Mother during illness  
• trust the Pale Shepherd with burial rites

Few see this as contradiction.

Faith in the kingdom is less about strict doctrine and more about **turning to the right belief when the moment demands it**.
